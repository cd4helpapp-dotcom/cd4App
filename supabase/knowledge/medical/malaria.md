## What malaria is
Malaria is a life-threatening but preventable and curable disease caused by a parasite. It spreads mainly through bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes, and does not spread from person to person.

## Malaria symptoms
Common early symptoms are fever, headache and chills, usually starting 10 to 15 days after an infected bite. They can be mild and look like other fevers, so early testing matters.

## Malaria warning signs
Get emergency care right away for extreme tiredness, reduced consciousness, repeated convulsions, difficulty breathing, dark or bloody urine, yellow eyes or skin, or abnormal bleeding. Untreated falciparum malaria can become severe and fatal within 24 hours.

## Who is at higher risk of malaria
Infants, children under 5, pregnant women and girls, travellers, and people with HIV or AIDS. Malaria in pregnancy can cause premature delivery or low birth weight.

## Malaria testing and treatment
WHO recommends confirming every suspected case with a blood test, either microscopy or a rapid diagnostic test. Malaria always needs medicine, chosen by a doctor according to the malaria type, drug resistance, the patient's weight or age, and pregnancy.

## Preventing malaria
Use bed nets, repellents containing DEET, IR3535 or Icaridin after dusk, protective clothes and window screens. Travellers should see a doctor several weeks before going to a malaria area.
