## What diarrhoea is
Diarrhoea is passing 3 or more loose or liquid stools a day, or more often than is normal for the person. It is usually caused by an infection from contaminated water or food, or poor hygiene. Loose stools in breastfed babies do not count.

## Dehydration signs
Some dehydration: restlessness or irritability, sunken eyes, drinking eagerly. Severe dehydration: unusual sleepiness or unconsciousness, sunken eyes, unable to drink or drinking poorly, and pinched skin that returns very slowly. Severe dehydration is an emergency.

## Diarrhoea treatment
Oral rehydration solution (ORS), made with clean water, salt and sugar, replaces lost water and salts. A 10 to 14 day course of zinc tablets helps children recover faster. Keep eating nutritious food and continue breastfeeding during an episode.

## When diarrhoea needs a doctor
See a doctor if there is blood in the stool, signs of dehydration, or diarrhoea lasting 14 days or longer.

## Preventing diarrhoea
Safe drinking water, good sanitation, handwashing with soap, good food hygiene, exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months, and rotavirus vaccination.
