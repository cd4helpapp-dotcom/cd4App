## What TB is
Tuberculosis is a bacterial infection that most often affects the lungs. It spreads through the air when someone with TB coughs, sneezes or spits. It is preventable and curable.

## TB symptoms
A long-lasting cough, sometimes with blood, chest pain, weakness, tiredness, weight loss, fever and night sweats. Some people with TB disease have no symptoms but can still spread it. See a doctor for a prolonged cough, fever or unexplained weight loss.

## Who is at higher risk of TB
Babies and children, and people with diabetes, a weak immune system such as from HIV, undernutrition, tobacco use or harmful alcohol use. People who live or work with someone who has TB should get screened.

## TB testing and treatment
WHO recommends rapid diagnostic tests first, which can give initial results within 48 hours. TB is treated with antibiotics taken daily for 4 to 6 months. Stopping early or without medical advice can make the bacteria resistant to the medicines, so complete the full course.

## Preventing TB spread
If you have TB, wear a mask, cover your mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing, avoid close contact, and dispose of sputum and used tissues properly. Preventive treatment can stop infection becoming disease. The BCG vaccine is given to babies or small children in some countries.
